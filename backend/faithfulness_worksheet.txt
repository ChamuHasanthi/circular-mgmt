--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: The Central Bank of Sri Lanka has issued an addendum to
  Circular No. 04 of 2024 on Relief Measures to Assist the Affected Small
  and Medium Enterprises (SMEs). This circular aims to provide relief
  measures for SMEs affected by the economic situation, ensuring effective
  implementation across all licensed banks. Key Points: - Licensed banks
  must establish a Relief Banking Unit to extend and monitor relief
  measures under Circular No. 04 of 2024. - Banks may reschedule credit
  facilities for eligible borrowers up to ten years on a case-by-case
  basis, considering repayment capacity and an acceptable revival plan. -
  Licensed banks are requested to establish a transparent grievance-
  handling mechanism with respect to disputes between the bank and
  borrower regarding auctioned properties. - Banks must report details of
  borrowers who approached them to avail relief measures from 15.12.2024
  to the reporting date. - The existing Business Revival Units established
  under Circular No. 02 of 2024 will continue for their intended purpose.

[3] Circular 01/2021  (id 22)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: Licensed commercial banks and licensed specialised banks are
  required to suspend recovery actions against Small and Medium Enterprise
  (SME) Paddy Millers for a period of six months due to the COVID-19
  pandemic. This suspension is aimed at supporting SME Paddy Millers
  amidst the Government's initiatives. The suspension applies from January
  1, 2021.  Key Points: - Licensed banks must suspend recovery actions
  against SME Paddy Millers until June 30, 2021. - Recovery actions can be
  suspended on condition that a debt re-payment agreement is reached
  between the bank and the borrower. - In cases where resolutions for
  recovery have already been passed, auctioning of assets will be
  suspended until June 30, 2021. - Licensed banks must defer passing new
  resolutions under relevant Acts until June 30, 2021. - Borrowers with
  on-going litigations in courts relating to recovery can enter into an
  agreement by submitting an affidavit to the Courts.

[4] Circular 01/2022  (id 23)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: The Central Bank of Sri Lanka has issued guidelines for
  licensed banks to establish Post COVID-19 Revival Units to assist
  underperforming and non-performing borrowers affected by the pandemic.
  The purpose is to revive viable businesses and contribute to economic
  development.  Key Points: - Licensed banks must formulate a revival and
  rehabilitation policy approved by the Board of Directors, including a
  mandate for establishment, scope of activities, revival mechanism, and
  other necessary measures. - The Revival Unit shall be headed by a Key
  Management Personnel with sufficient authority and seniority, and
  licensed banks must ensure adequate staffing and resources for the Unit.
  - Licensed banks may consider establishing Units at large branches or
  regional offices if they have more than 50 bank branches; otherwise, the
  Unit will be centrally located. - The Revival Unit shall actively liaise
  with branches and other business units to identify borrowers requiring
  rehabilitation assistance and develop rehabilitation proposals. -
  Licensed banks must not consider borrowers that have defaulted due to
  diversion of funds, mismanagement, or unviable projects for revival,
  unless they are classified as non-performing prior to 01 April 2020 or
  under litigation. - The Revival Unit shall conduct awareness programs,
  provide credit counselling and business advisory services, and assist
  businesses in reaching out to potential investors. - Licensed banks must
  adopt accounting treatment for facilities considered under the Unit as
  per Sri Lanka Accounting Standards and related Circulars/guidelines
  issued by CBSL. - Licensed banks must report progress of rehabilitation
  proposals availing concessions under Post COVID-19 Revival Units to the
  bank's borrowers at the end of each quarter, within 15 working days.

[5] Circular 02/2022  (id 24)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: The Central Bank of Sri Lanka has issued a circular to provide
  concessions to affected borrowers due to extraordinary macroeconomic
  circumstances. Licensed banks are required to implement these
  concessions to help businesses and individuals recover from the impact
  of the crisis. Key Points: - Licensed banks must provide appropriate
  concessions for performing credit facilities of individuals or
  businesses whose income or business has been adversely affected by the
  current macroeconomic conditions, on a case-by-case basis, for a period
  of six months from the date of this Circular. - Concessions may include
  a grace period for capital or interest, restructuring of credit
  facilities, or any other concession. The interest rate applicable for
  concessions granted to non-performing credit facilities shall not exceed
  15.5%. - Licensed banks must devise a suitable mechanism to structure
  the repayment plan for regular installment loans with an extended
  tenure, not exceeding the contracted instalment value of the existing
  credit facility. - Borrowers who wish to fully settle any of the
  existing credit facilities without charging additional fees shall be
  given the opportunity to do so. - Licensed banks must suspend recovery
  actions against all credit facilities that have been classified as non-
  performing on or after 01.01.2020, until 31.12.2022. - Borrowers who are
  willing for an early settlement of credit facilities must make a request
  to the respective licensed bank on or before 30.09.2022. - Licensed
  banks shall report the details of concessions availed by their borrowers
  to the Bank Supervision Department, as at each month end, within 15
  working days, commencing from 31 July 2022. - Borrowers who are willing
  for an early settlement of credit facilities must make a request to the
  respective licensed bank on or before 30.09.2022. - Licensed banks shall
  not decline new loan applications from borrowers solely based on adverse
  CRIB records.

[6] Circular 04/2018  (id 25)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: This circular outlines guidelines for licensed banks in Sri
  Lanka to adopt the Sri Lanka Accounting Standard (SLFRS 9) on Financial
  Instruments. The aim is to establish consistent and prudent practices in
  credit risk management, impairment of financial assets, and regulatory
  reporting. Key Points: - Licensed banks must calculate expected credit
  losses for financial instruments measured at amortised cost or fair
  value through other comprehensive income, using data from at least five-
  year periods. - Credit facilities with contractual payments more than 30
  days past due are classified as Stage 2, while those with payments over
  90 days past due are classified as Stage 3. - Licensed banks must
  maintain standardized processes and detailed checklists to assess and
  identify SPPI features of their products and contracts. - The aggregate
  amount of impairment allowances should be adequate and consistent with
  the objectives of SLFRS 9. - Licensed banks shall submit all periodical
  information, including web-based statutory returns, to CBSL in
  accordance with existing regulations within 30 days after the end of
  each quarter. - The first day impact arising from the adoption of SLFRS
  9 must be disclosed in financial statements and reported to CBSL within
  30 days after the end of each quarter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: The Central Bank of Sri Lanka has issued an amendment to
  Circular No. 04 of 2018 regarding the adoption of Sri Lanka Accounting
  Standard — SLFRS 9: Financial Instruments by licensed banks. This
  circular aims to establish consistent practices in the adoption of this
  standard during exceptional circumstances.  Key Points: - Licensed banks
  must apply a minimum Loan Loss Coverage (LGD) of 10 per cent when
  computing expected losses for the year 2021. - The amendment inserts
  Guideline 2.5(c) into Circular No. 04 of 2018, which permits this
  exception. - This change applies to all licensed banks in Sri Lanka. -
  The amendment is effective immediately and supersedes previous
  guidelines. - Banks must update their accounting practices accordingly.
  - Compliance with the amended guideline is mandatory for all licensed
  banks. - The Central Bank will monitor compliance with the new
  guideline.

[8] Circular 02/2025  (id 28)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY:
  Overview: Licensed banks in Sri Lanka are required to report IT and
  cybersecurity incidents to the Central Bank of Sri Lanka (CBSL) to
  ensure operational resilience and safeguard customer information and
  assets.  Key Points: - Licensed banks must report IT and cybersecurity
  incidents, including intrusion/hacking, malware, ransomware, phishing,
  and online scams, within 2 hours of detection to the Director, Bank
  Supervision Department via techrisk.bsd@cbsl.lk. - Quarterly reports on
  IT and cybersecurity incidents must be submitted by licensed banks
  within 15 days following the end of each quarter to the Director, Bank
  Supervision Department via techrisk.bsd@cbsl.lk. - Licensed banks are
  required to submit detailed reports on IT and cybersecurity incidents
  within 14 working days of detection to the Director, Bank Supervision
  Department via techrisk.bsd@cbsl.lk. - The report must include
  information on the incident, business impact, data and information
  security impact, stakeholders' and customers' impact, reputational
  impact, regulatory or legal impact, financial impact, corrective actions
  taken, and target dates for corrective actions. - Licensed banks are
  required to submit a preliminary impact assessment in summary within 2
  hours of detection to the Director, Bank Supervision Department via
  techrisk.bsd@cbsl.lk.
